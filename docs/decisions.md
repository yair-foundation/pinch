They were made and numbered in the GenUI repository, where Pinch began as the `/pin` route, so the numbers run D58–D67; the GenUI decisions (D1–D57, D56 the handbook and D57 its references among them) and the links to `product.md` don't apply here. Where an entry says "the handbook password", read `ADMIN_PASSWORD`; where it says `PIN_HOSTS` or "GenUI's own host", that host switching is gone: this repository serves Pinch at `/` and nothing else. Code links point at [architecture.md](architecture.md).

Rules themselves live in [design.md](design.md) and [architecture.md](architecture.md); this page only records each choice and its reason. New decisions go at the top, numbered on from D98.

## Settled

- **2026-10-21 — D98 One offer: 2,000 credits for $19.99; an update costs 20 credits and 3 a note, close-ups included (user decisions, after the cost and crop benchmarks). Decided; not built.** A reading costs a fixed part for each update plus a little for each note ([cost-bench.md](cost-bench.md)), so a price per note can't follow it; credits priced the same two ways can. Amends D96: credits replace note readings, and the reading sees close-ups.
  - **One offer.** A pack of 2,000 credits for $19.99 that never expires, bought again for more. No tiers, plans, subscriptions or other pack sizes.
  - **What an update costs:** 20 credits, plus 3 for each new or changed note, its close-up included. An update is the author's click on Update rules, however many calls it takes behind it. The button shows the price ("Update rules · 12 notes · 56 credits") and that is what it takes: retries, caching and the calls behind it are Pinch's to absorb. If an update stops partway, the credits for the notes it didn't read come back.
  - **Free: 200 credits,** priced the same way, for a browser and then the account it signs in to: 50 notes in two updates, or eight single-note updates. No separate count of free notes.
  - **At zero balance** browsing, editing by hand, copying and the agent connection keep working (D96); only updates stop.
  - **Close-ups for everyone.** Each note goes to the reading with its close-up: in the crop benchmark, 5 of 9 vague notes and 12 of 18 spot notes read right, against 0 and 6 with words alone, for about $0.002 to $0.005 a note ([rules-bench.md](rules-bench.md#close-ups-in-the-reading-d96)). Anonymous readers too: before their first update, the privacy dialog says their notes and the close-ups of their marks go to the AI provider. Signing in is no substitute for saying so. Close-ups make a reading more plausible, not proven (three notes were read wrong with them), so every interpretation still waits for the author (D91).
  - **What it pays for, as tested.** A single-note update against a full library costs about $0.05 to $0.08 and takes 23 credits, about $0.23 of the buyer's balance; a 30-note update costs about $0.18 to $0.32 and takes 110 credits, about $1.10. A pack holds 86 single-note updates, 57 of five notes (285 notes) or 18 of thirty (540 notes). At about $0.08 each, 86 single-note updates cost about $6.88 of AI against about $18.49 left after Lemon Squeezy's fee (assumed), before hosting, suggestions, storage and refunds. A launch hypothesis, to be judged by real use: whether people understand the credits, use the guidance and buy.
  - **To build, changed from D96's list:** the ledger counts credits; the price is worked out before the update and taken whole; the free 200 replace the free 50 readings; the webhook grants 2,000 for each paid order and takes back what is left of a refunded one; the page sends each note's close-up with its words; the privacy dialog, the terms and AGENTS.md's privacy rule say so.
  - **Earlier buyers keep what they bought (user decision, 2026-10-21).** D81 sold one payment of $19 for Pinch as it was and every update for twelve months, with daily fair-use limits on the AI shared by everyone. Those buyers keep that functionality under those limits; "twelve months of updates" isn't twelve months of use, so their date is no reason to move them to credits. They can switch to credits if they prefer. Converting their purchase into 2,000 credits was turned down: a fixed number of credits can be less than an ongoing allowance, and a simpler build is no reason to take that from them. New paid capabilities may be priced apart, as long as what they bought stays theirs. New buyers buy credits. Production's logs show one earlier order, most likely the owner's own test (Open, above).

- **2026-10-20 — D97 The reading sends its library one rule a line and caches its instructions (the cost benchmark).** Most of what a reading of a few notes costs was fixed (D96, [cost-bench.md](cost-bench.md)): about 6,700 tokens of instructions on every call, and about 84 tokens a library rule as indented JSON.
  - **One rule a line.** Each library rule goes as one line: its id, `[dismissed]`, `[interpreted]`, its topics, where it holds, its words and any open question, after a line saying how to read them (`ruleLine`). An author's line breaks become spaces, so a rule can't pass for two. The notes go as JSON on one line. A library of 200 rules drops from 16,793 tokens to 7,835.
  - **The instructions are cached.** They are the same for every author, so they come from the cache whenever anyone has read in the last five minutes. The library isn't: nearly every reading that adds a rule changes it, and writing it to the cache costs a quarter more than sending it.
  - **Checked.** The rules benchmark read its cases with the prompt before and after in the same run ([rules-bench.md](rules-bench.md)): as good or better on every tuning count (explicit preferences 42/42 against 40/42, corrections 2.5 against 2.8, vague notes right 17/24 against 13/24, every conflict and strengthening kept), within noise on the held-out case. The cases' libraries are small, so a large library's conflicts are still to test.
  - **Cost.** One note against 200 rules: $0.074 cold, $0.045 with the instructions cached, from $0.10 to $0.13. A pack of 500 in the assumed mix against a full library: $7.67 to $11.55, from $15.53 to $18.97. One note at a time against a full library still costs $22.67 to $37.20 a pack.

- **2026-10-20 — D96 Free readings, then prepaid note packs; Pinch reads only when asked (user decisions, after an outside review and research on pricing). Decided; not built.** The paywall showed a visitor two rules and hid the rest, so nobody saw whether Pinch's guidance improved their agent's work before paying. Now new AI reading is what's paid for. Guidance already made stays usable, and how it is delivered is never gated. Amends D81 (what $19 buys, twelve months of updates, a refund ending access), D87 (the two-rule preview, reading on opening Your rules, rules moving into the account on buying) and D90 (credits were paused).
  - **Free: 50 note readings a person.** Every rule they make is shown and can be edited and copied as a prompt. A free Google account keeps that library in the account and connects an agent over MCP. The two-rule preview, its placeholders and its own ledger go. Without an account, readings are counted per browser by a signed id, not by the IP hash, which would make an office share 50; signing in carries the count into the account, 50 in all, and the browser's rules move in then.
  - **Paid: prepaid packs that never expire,** bought again for more. The launch hypothesis is $19.99 for 500 readings (first 1,000 at $19, cut to 500 before any measurement, for more room for processing costs and a smaller amount to buy); until the size is measured (Open, above) the wording is "Includes [validated quantity] AI note readings". At zero balance the library, editing, copying and the agent connection all keep working; only new readings stop. The free 50 are spent first, then packs, oldest first.
  - **What uses a reading:** one new or changed note read into the rules. A note is known by its image and its words, so an edited note is a new one. Naming rules, editing them by hand, merging inside a reading, failed calls, retries, browsing and every MCP call use none.
  - **Pinch reads only when asked.** Opening Your rules no longer reads on its own. New and changed notes wait, and one button, "Update rules · 12 notes", reads them, with what is left beside it. Looking around never spends, rewording a note a few times costs one reading when the author is done, and notes read together share a call's fixed cost.
  - **Why the call matters more than the note.** Every call sends the same instructions and foundation, up to the newest 200 rules, and the new notes. At Opus 5.5's $4 in and $20 out per million tokens, that is about $0.006 a note when 30 are read against a full library, but about $0.11 for one note alone. These are estimates from the prompt's size, not measurements. Caching the fixed prompt and keeping the library a stable prefix will help, but a returning author's cache is often cold, so the pack has to pay without it.
  - **The ledger:** a credit document per account in the Blob store, changed by compare-and-swap like the AI ledgers. Grants are keyed by Lemon Squeezy's order id, so a repeated webhook can't grant twice; each reading is reserved, then settled by its request id, so retried or simultaneous calls can't spend twice.
  - **Refunds:** the whole payment back within 14 days, however much was used, for v1; abuse is watched before any threshold is added. A refund takes away what is left of that pack. What was used is Pinch's cost, never a debt, so a later pack gives all it says. The library stays.
  - **Suggestions:** for accounts with readings left, under the daily cap. Each costs under a tenth of a cent on Haiku, but a balance that never reaches zero doesn't bound them: an ongoing cost, accepted and watched.
  - **Storage:** rules are kept while the account exists. Screenshots are capped at 200 MB for a free account and 2 GB for a paid one; the 300 MB limit per batch stays. At the storage rate Vercel lists ($0.023 a GB-month), a full 2 GB is about $0.55 a year, before transfers and requests. A free account with no sign-in and no MCP use for 12 months loses its screenshots, after notice by email and a way to keep them; its rules stay. The clean-up stays off until those emails actually go out.
  - **What Pinch claims:** the reading sees words only, so Pinch says it reads your notes and asks when they're vague (D90, D91), not that it sees what you liked; the agent is the one that sees the close-ups (D94).
  - **Next, in order:** measure the cost where the API key is set (real input, output, cache-write and cache-read tokens, retries included); test crops on a small set of representative notes; choose the pack size from realistic use, people reading one or five notes at a time as well as 30; then build, and align the pricing, terms and privacy copy with what ships.
  - **To build:** the ledger and its counter; the browser id; Update rules in place of reading on open; removing the preview; free accounts allowed into the account's storage, keys and MCP (`access.ts`); grants per order in the webhook; prompt caching; the storage caps, last activity (MCP use counts) and the clean-up, off until its email works (Pinch sends no email yet); the terms (twelve months, a refund ending access, and the exports and collection download it still names); the privacy dialog and AGENTS.md's privacy rule, since free accounts will hold screenshots.

- **2026-10-20 — D95 Notes as short items, rules with titles, a clearer rules page (the user's 13 changes).**
  - **A note's two fields are lists.** "What works" and "What doesn't" became "What to learn" and "What to avoid", each a list of short items, to make a note easy to write and scan. Enter starts a new item (split at the caret, with "Another thing to learn" as its placeholder), Backspace at an item's start joins it to the one above, the arrows move between items, and pasted lines become items. Each written item has a checkbox whose only use is to select it for Delete: it isn't a to-do, so a selected item is tinted, never struck through or faded. The model is unchanged: a field is still a string, one item per line, so sessions, suggestions (Tab still takes one into the first item), saving and the reading need nothing new. Exports keep the items one per line in `notes.json` and join them "a; b" in the README, the rules text and the agent's answers (`itemsOf`, `itemsLine`).
  - **The note panel:** the "Note 1 · dot" heading is gone; Delete note sits under the note, and Done is the panel's full-width primary button right above Back and Next image (moved from the top right, D93). Source, the two lists and Tags (was "This note is about") each lead with their icon in a small rounded box, with more room under the label; a rule's notes show their thumbs the same way, under their title line. Fields lost their focus ring for a quieter border change.
  - **Every rule has a short title,** two to five words, written by the AI. The reading writes it with each new rule (`title`); rules without one, written before or rewritten by the author, are named by a small model (`POST /api/pin/learn/titles`, `PIN_TITLE_MODEL`, Haiku), only their words sent, for buyers, on the reading's ledger, each rule asked about once a visit. A rewritten rule loses its title and is named again. A visitor's hidden rules get no title, so their words never show (D87).
  - **The rules page's type has a scale:** the page title 24px medium, a topic group 13px muted, a rule's title 15px medium, its words 14px muted; "1 rule" is gone (what waits is said in the line under the title). The rule card is laid out in flow, the pencil beside the title, so its field, its Source row with Remove and its notes all share the card's edges.

- **2026-10-20 — D94 A rule's close-ups reach the agent, with where the spot is as numbers (the user: "crops with boxes over MCP").** Since D93 the agent got only a note's words and its image's name, no picture and no position; the batch zip that carried both was gone. Numbers alone mean nothing to an agent that can't see the screenshot, so they come with the picture.
  - **A third tool, `get_rule_evidence`,** takes a rule's number (R3) and returns its notes, up to eight. Each comes with its crop as a JPEG image, with Pinch's marker drawn in, and a line of JSON saying where the spot is.
  - **The box** is in pixels from the top-left corner, the convention OpenCV, PIL and COCO share: a dot `{x, y}`, an area `{x, y, w, h}`, an arrow `{x, y, x2, y2}`. It is given in the close-up's own pixels (`closeUp`, with the part of the screenshot the crop shows) and in the screenshot's (`screenshot`, with the same as fractions). The answer says screenshot pixels aren't CSS pixels. CSS positions and HTML image maps were turned down: CSS units claim layout facts a screenshot doesn't hold, and no agent reads image maps.
  - **Rules keep one number in every pick,** their number among all rules, so the number get_design_rules shows is the one get_rule_evidence takes; get_design_rules says the close-ups are there.
  - **Only the needed bytes are read.** The crops sit in each batch's zip in the account. The server reads its tail, its directory and the wanted entries by HTTP ranges on a presigned URL (`src/lib/pin/zip-range.ts`), not the whole zip, and the whole zip once if the store ignores ranges.
  - **Privacy:** a buyer's own screenshots, kept in their account (D81, D87), go to their own agent, which holds their key. The privacy dialog says so. Nothing is logged but the method.
  - The copied prompt stays words only: a chat paste carries no images.

- **2026-10-20 — D91 Every interpretation waits for the author (the goal: "users should approve that interpretation before it becomes saved guidance"; the user kept the card to the pencil, D88).** Since D90 a reading of vague words went to the agent at once, marked as Pinch's reading, unless it came with a question. - **2026-10-20 — D90 Pinch reads notes with an expert design foundation, kept apart from the rules, and asks when it can't tell (the assessment: "keep the expert foundation … the foundation doesn't resolve ambiguity by itself … Pinch should preserve that uncertainty or ask a focused question"; the user: "for every retained principle, define when it applies, its exceptions, and what evidence would justify using it … keep that foundation separate from the collection: expert knowledge helps explain the observation; the user's annotations establish the preference").** Notes are shorthand ("quiet", "heavy", "I like this heading"). Before this, the reading dropped such notes without a word, or guessed.
  - **The foundation.** `src/lib/pin/foundation.ts` holds sixteen principles in Pinch's own words. Each says where it applies, where it gives way, the evidence in notes that justifies reading a note through it, and its sources. With them comes the shorthand designers use and the principles each word points at.
  - **Sources, each licence checked, none copied.** GenUI's design foundations and review method (the Yair Foundation's own; no licence file). Anthropic's frontend-design skill (Apache 2.0). Vercel's Web Interface Guidelines (MIT), behind the web-design-guidelines skill. From the guidelines, only judgment a screenshot shows: no code checks, and none of what they call Vercel's own brand choices.
  - **Kept apart.** A rule stores only the ids of the principles that helped read it, never their words. A principle never becomes a rule, widens one, or adds a size, ratio or colour the notes don't give. The author sees them under a rule's notes ("Pinch read your notes with these design principles; your notes make it your rule"); the export names them under "Explained by", and an agent is told never to apply one further than its rule.
  - **Stated or interpreted.** Each new rule says whether the notes state it or it is Pinch's reading of vaguer words, shown as "Pinch’s reading: …" and handed to the agent as such. A reading has to commit to something an agent would do differently.
  - **Asking.** Where the words could mean designs that differ and nothing else settles it, Pinch asks one short question with two to four answers instead of guessing. The rule waits, out of exports and the agent connection, until the author picks an answer (one click) or writes their own ("Something else" opens the pencil). Rewriting a rule makes it the author's own words: no reading, no question; the principles stay, since they explain the notes.
  - **Organising.** Each principle has a short name to browse by (Typography, Hierarchy, Spacing, Tone and words…), and the rules page slices by them in a "By principle" row, beside topics and tags. A line under "Your tags" says that a tag keeps a rule to a project or client and that a rule with no tag is the author's own taste; the untagged slice is called "General". The pencil can now remove an outdated rule. It is kept as dismissed, so Pinch never writes it again, with Undo in the notice; the card itself still shows only the pencil (D88).
  - **Product-led, bottom up (the user: "this tool should be bottom up product led growth").** One designer gets value free before anyone pays, so a visitor's two preview rules are now the surest ones: none waiting on a question, then those drawn from the most notes. Teams, credits and checkout changes stay paused, as the assessment advised.
  - **Tested.** A benchmark (`scripts/rules-bench/`, results in [rules-bench.md](rules-bench.md)) compares a plain prompt, Pinch before the foundation and Pinch with it. It runs on hand-written cases with keys, plus a held-out case each round.
    - On the tuning cases the foundation needed the fewest corrections every round: 3.0 a reading in the last, against 4.9 and 5.6. It handled vague notes far better than Pinch before it (15 of 24 against 4), with unsupported assumptions about as low (1.8 against 1.5; the plain prompt made 6.8).
    - On the held-out cases it beat or tied Pinch before it, and was mixed against the plain prompt.
    - On fresh tasks, any rules lifted an agent's work well above none, but the three rule sets stayed within noise of each other. What every set missed is what only the designer can give.
  - **Cost.** A reading of a dozen notes costs about $0.065, against $0.029 before: still well inside the ledgers.

- **2026-10-20 — D89 Your agent fetches your rules itself: Pinch is an MCP server (goal: "apply a collection: give an agent the relevant guidance for the current task").** An export is a copy that goes stale.
