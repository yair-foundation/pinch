Code links point at [architecture.md](architecture.md).

Rules themselves live in [design.md](design.md) and [architecture.md](architecture.md); this page only records each choice and its reason. New decisions go at the top, numbered on from D143.

## Settled

- **2026-10-28 — D143 The five-second count runs once a visit, before the first recording; pausing and resuming are immediate (the user: "remove the 5 to 0 countdown when the user hit record (the space bar), keep it only for the first time in the session … when user pause and resume the recording - it should happens immidialty"). Amends D131.** The count is there to let the author settle before speaking the first time; once they're talking, waiting five seconds after every pause breaks the flow it was meant to protect. So the first R (or Space) of a visit counts down as before, and every later one, a resume or a new take, records at once. "A visit" is the browser tab: it's kept in `sessionStorage` (`pinch-counted`), so a reload doesn't bring the count back and a new tab does. A count stopped with Esc doesn't count, so the next R counts again. The legend and the shortcuts say "Record, or resume".
- **2026-10-28 — D142 A PDF's pages draw with every image in them (the user: "the pdf's that i have attached are missing the images in them"). Amends D139.** pdf.js draws most of a page with its own code, but JPEG 2000 and JBIG2 images need its WebAssembly decoders, which it loads from `wasmUrl`; without that it skipped them ("OpenJPEG failed to initialize") and the page came in with a blank where the picture was. Preview and Acrobat often save photos and scans that way. Colour profiles (`iccUrl`), the CMaps for Chinese, Japanese and Korean text (`cMapUrl`) and the standard fonts for text that embeds none (`standardFontDataUrl`) are set too, for the same reason. They come from jsdelivr at the version of `pdfjs-dist` installed, as the voice runtime does (D140); the PDF itself still never leaves the browser. Checked with a three-page PDF holding a JPEG, a JPEG 2000 and a JPEG with a Display P3 profile: before, the JPEG 2000 page was blank; after, all three drew in full.

No Undo: the zips go, so "Reset your rules?" asks first, with the counts.
  - **What stays:** the account, the agent's key (the agent stays connected, to an empty library), the credits, the author's briefs and roles for their products, the free plan's product, and the versions exported (D128), so an agent holding an old copy hears that its rules are gone. The agent's work reports (D120) stay as a record.
  - **Shown** when there is anything to reset, as a quiet red button under Batches.
- **2026-10-27 — D140 On the live site, the browser reads the words itself, free (the user: "Bring voice to get-pinch.io for free with speech-to-text that runs in the browser, so anyone can narrate there"). Amends D131, which ruled this out, and D136, which stays for the author's own Mac.** Since the reel replaced typing (D131), the live site, with no speech-to-text, made no notes at all. A cloud service costs per minute; the browser costs nothing, and the audio never leaves it.
  - **Whisper base, English, in a worker.** `onnx-community/whisper-base.en_timestamped` through transformers.js (`@huggingface/transformers`), loaded only when a take is read, in a Web Worker (`whisper.worker.ts`) so the page stays responsive. It gives each word its time (`return_timestamps: 'word'`), 30-second windows with a 5-second overlap. English only, as the voice tags are (D131); the English model heard "cramped" where the multilingual one of the same size heard "crammed". On the GPU where the browser has WebGPU (encoder at full precision, decoder in eight bits, about 136 MB), in WebAssembly otherwise or when the GPU fails (both in eight bits, about 77 MB).
  - **Fetched once, from Hugging Face.** The model comes from huggingface.co and the WebAssembly runtime from jsdelivr, a version transformers.js pins, on the first take; the browser caches both (`transformers-cache`), so later takes start at once. The end card says so while it downloads ("Getting the voice model, once (up to 140 MB): 42%"), then "Reading the words…". D131 ruled the browser out for this download and for weaker accuracy on design words; one download per browser, after a first take, is a fair price for free and private, and a cloud engine can still replace it behind the same `wordsOf`.
  - **Which engine.** The reel asks `/api/pin/voice` first: where the server has whisper.cpp (D136) it uses that; everywhere else, the browser's. The audio is decoded in the page to 16 kHz mono and handed to the worker; it isn't sent anywhere. The privacy dialog and the terms say so.
  - **Checked** on a production build with the server's speech-to-text off, as on the live site: a spoken take read into words with their times in 11 seconds, the model download included, the voice tags applied and the critique made a note on the spot the pointer rested on; no request carried the audio; the model cached.

- **2026-10-27 — D137 The words become notes where the pointer was, with no model yet (the user: "turn the heard words into notes next, using the pointer to place them", after "i don't want to spend money on APIs for now"). Amends D131 for now; D136 reads the words.**
  - **A claim is a sentence,** ended by a full stop or a pause (`SENTENCE_PAUSE`), split again at "but", "however", "although", "though", "whereas" and "yet", with the voice tags taken out first (`parseVoiceTags`). Fillers alone ("um", "okay so") make nothing. D131 has a model split the words and write each note's what works and what to avoid; until Pinch pays for that, each claim is one note in the author's own words (`src/lib/pin/voice-notes.ts`), which the reading of notes into rules reads as it reads typed ones.
  - **Works or doesn't, by its words:** praise ("love", "clean", "calm") counts for it and faults ("too", "cramped", "missing") against it, each turned round by a "not" just before; "not bad" is praise, and a bare negation is a fault. Even, as a plain description is, it says what works: the author chose to say it. It can be wrong, so the end card's Works or Doesn't work beside each note switches it with a click. Topics come from the words too ("spacing" is Layout, "font" Visual).
  - **The pointer places it, as D131 set out.** A circle drawn while it was said (the path turning through about 315°) makes an area round it, a little outside. "Move" or "align" with the pointer resting on two spots makes an arrow from the first to the last. "This", "here", "these", "that" or "there" makes a dot where the pointer rested as it was said, or else wherever it was. A rest with none of those words makes a dot on the longest rest. Otherwise the note is about the whole screen (an area over all of it). The pointer is read from 0.4 s before the claim to 0.2 s after, as pointing leads speech, and only on the screen it was said over.
  - **The voice tags heard are applied, in the order said.** A tag and a source go on the screen they were said over and carry on to the screens first shown after it until another is named, but never over a tag or source a screen already had. "Star this" or "best example" stars the note under the pointer, or the last one; "star the second one" the second of the screen's spoken notes. Topics said go to the last note. "Drop that", "drop the second one" and "scratch that" remove one; "redo this screen" removes what was said on it before; "not that, this" moves the last note to the pointer. The role (D110) belongs to the tag's set on Your rules and isn't set from here yet.
  - **Notes keep a new origin, `voice`,** with the words in `quote`; the export says "said aloud, placed where the author pointed" and keeps the origin, so an import keeps it too.   - **Shown on the screen.** The middle screenshot draws its spoken notes, numbered in verdict colours: a dot, an area's outline, an arrow. The end card lists them by screen with what was heard. The screenshot's box is now its own shape (no letterboxing), so the pointer's fractions are fractions of the picture; before, a tall screenshot's were off. And the pointer is read against the screenshot on show, found by its place in the batch: the one ref handed from card to card could be left on a card that had turned away, and the pointer then went unrecorded.
