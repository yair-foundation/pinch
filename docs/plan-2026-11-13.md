# Plan: the harness as protected IP, a paid service, and a provable claim

2026-11-13. A plan, not a decision: nothing here is built, and each stage that changes a promise needs its own entry in [decisions.md](decisions.md) (next free number: D197) before it starts. It answers the eleven points the author asked for, in the order that builds: first what the one big improvement is, then the server, the licence, the pointer, the website and the services offer.

## The short version

1. **The best single improvement is a meaning pass.** Every note leaves Pinch able to stand alone: what was pointed at, what kind of thing it is, whether the note objects, approves or asks, and how sure Pinch is. Today half the notes are five words or fewer and mean nothing without the picture (the Guesty reader's count: 137 of 271).
2. **That pass runs on a server, and that is the IP.** The reading moves behind `/api/pin/` in three steps, text first, and the browser keeps only capture. Code that never ships cannot be copied.
3. **Every change is scored on a kept set of real takes.** The set, with its labels, is the part a competitor cannot rebuild from reading the product.
4. **Money comes from two places:** harness projects for companies (now, on the Guesty work) and a subscription for the server reading and the check (after stage 2).
5. **The website claims two things, each with a number and its method:** fewer corrections with Pinch than without, and more pointed notes a minute than marking by hand.

## 1. The one improvement that raises the bar (points 6 and 8)

**"Pinch understands what you mean, not just what you say"** is not true yet, and the Guesty reader's feedback (`docs/reviews/pinch-package-feedback-2026-11-13.md` in the deck harness) says exactly where:

- 137 of 271 notes are five words or fewer: "Avoid this", "Off-brand", "Same here".
- 149 of 328 spots have no text under them, so the picture is the only source.
- The "Not sure" list flagged nine fragments and missed the six notes that were really misheard ("Too much more, Gene" for too much margin).
- Approval and objection looked the same. Word lists took 150 of 289 notes as saying what works; 4 of the 21 it was sure of were right (D195).
- One note carried five claims and two spots.

Word lists have reached their ceiling: `voice-notes.ts` already holds sets for praise, fault, wishes, negation, fillers and danglers, and each real take finds new exceptions. The step up is to read each note **with its picture in view**.

**The meaning pass.** For each note, a model sees the close-up, the marked screen, the words as said, the note before and after, and the gesture. It writes:

| Field | Example | Fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | "the filled coral button, top right, 'Book a demo'" | spots with no text; "this", "here", "same here" |
| Kind | button, title, picture, icon, logo, margin, empty area | "say what kind of thing is there" |
| Stance | objection, approval, question, correction of note 3 | approval lost among objections |
| The claim, whole | "This button's colour is off-brand" from "off-brand" | notes that mean nothing alone |
| One claim to one spot | five claims split across their two spots | claims that lost their spot |
| Sure or not, and why | "margin, Gene" is not a phrase; likely "margin" | the wrong "Not sure" list |

The words as said always stay beside it (D157, D168): the pass adds, it never replaces.

**Ask, don't guess.** Where the pass is unsure, the end card asks the author before Export: the close-up, what Pinch heard, what it thinks was meant, one key to accept and one to say it again. A person confirming takes a second and is right; a model guessing is neither. This is the cheapest part and may be worth building first, on the fragments already flagged.

**How we know it worked.** Three numbers on the kept takes (section 3): notes a fresh agent can act on without opening a picture (today about half); stance right (today near chance); misheard notes caught (today 0 of 6 on the Guesty take). Then the taste harness again: corrections back, and changes asked for a screen.

**What it costs.** The close-up goes to Anthropic for the pass. D98 already allows close-ups of marks to go there, never stored, for a signed-in person who has been told. So the pass is for signed-in people who switch it on, and the free export stays as it is. At Haiku prices a note is a fraction of a cent; a 271-note take should cost well under a dollar, to be measured the way D178 measured the check.

## 2. The harness as deep IP, and the reading on the server (points 1 and 2)

**What can and cannot be protected.**

| Thing | Protectable? | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading a take (pieces, strokes, gesture to sentence, notes) | Yes | Runs on the server; never shipped |
| The meaning pass and its prompts | Yes | Server only |
| Which notes apply to a screen (retrieval) | Yes | Server only, behind the agent key |
| The kept takes and their labels | Yes | Never published; the strongest of the four |
| The check's instructions | Partly | Served per request with a key, versioned, metered. A person can still log what their agent receives |
| The package's format | No | It lands on the person's disk. Not worth defending |
| The capture UI | No | It is in the browser. Not worth defending |

Obfuscating browser code is not on the list: it slows a copy by a day and costs upkeep for good.

**Three stages, each shippable alone.**

**Stage 1: text to the server.** The browser still records, still reads speech, still holds the screenshots. It sends the take as Export already writes it (`pinch-take-*.json`: pointer path, screens shown, timed words; no audio, no image) to a new `POST /api/pin/read`, and gets notes with spots back. It draws the marked copies and close-ups itself from the spots.
- Moves: `speech-pieces` joining, `claimsOf`, `pointerOn` and `classify`, the tying of "Actually", the "Not sure" list, the layout of `notes.md`.
- For the text under a spot and the colour at a point, the browser sends what it read (words with their boxes, a few sampled colours), not the picture.
- Protects about 2,500 lines of the reading. Costs a round trip of a second or two, and takes away offline use.
- Not stored: read in memory, answered, dropped. Logged: a count and a duration, nothing said.
- To check first: the size of a 42-minute take's pointer path against Vercel's request limit; it may need packing.

**Stage 2: the meaning pass and the hosted check.** Signed-in, switched on.
- The pass of section 1 runs after stage 1's reading, on close-ups the browser sends for it.
- `pinch-check.md` becomes a short stub that calls two new MCP tools: `get_notes_for_screen` (send what kind of screen and what is on it; get back only the notes that apply, which is what the Guesty reader asked for when 40,000 tokens of notes met a small model) and `check_screen` (send a screenshot of the agent's work; get findings, each with the note it breaks). The local file stays for people who are not signed in, as it is today.
- This is what a subscription sells: a check that gets better without a new export, and notes that follow the person across projects.

**Stage 3: speech on the server, as a choice.** The 570 MB download and a strong machine are the largest barrier to a first take, and the small model gave nonsense on the live site (D159). Offer "Read in the cloud": audio goes up, is read and dropped, as Wispr Flow does ("Audio is discarded once it's transcribed"). On-device stays, and stays the default until the author decides otherwise. This is the biggest change to the privacy promise and is last for that reason.

**What every stage changes in the promise.** Today: nothing of a take leaves the browser (D157, D163). After stage 1: your screenshots and your voice stay in the browser; the words and the pointer's path are read on Pinch's server and not kept. The landing, the privacy dialog, `/terms` and the questions on the landing all say the old line and all change in the same commit. The rule in `AGENTS.md` changes with it.

**Reusing what exists.** The gate, agent keys, the MCP route and the daily caps are built. `READING_SHOWN` hides the old reading; this plan does not bring rules back (they lost 5 of 6, D155 and D156).

## 3. The kept takes: how every change is scored

A folder outside the repository (takes hold the author's words), with a small script in `scripts/` that reads every take with the current code and prints one table.

- **What is in it now:** the two gesture takes (labels said aloud, D185), the reviews of 336, 251 and 220 slides, the Guesty reader's findings as labels for the last.
- **What to add:** for each take, a hand-checked list of which spot each note should be on, its stance, and which notes were misheard. Two hours of the author's time a take, once.
- **Picks, kept too.** The author's 28 blind picks, and every later one, go in the set with the two screens each was between. Scoring a judge against a person's picks is how this field measures taste (section 8), and the pairs cost nothing more to keep.
- **Other people.** Every number today is one hand on one Mac. Three more people, one on a mouse and two on trackpads, each recording a gesture take and a ten-minute real review, with their agreement in writing.
- **The rule.** A change to the reading ships only if the table does not get worse. D185 to D195 were each checked this way by hand; this makes it one command.

## 4. The pointer: from 17 of 25 to something to defend (point 5)

Scoring first, since the score in use measures the wrong thing. `take-strokes.ts` asks "was a circle called a circle?". What matters to the reader is **"is the mark on the thing that was meant, and tied to the right sentence?"**. A scrub read as a rest may still mark the right spot. Score placement (the spot covers the element meant) and tying (the right sentence) on the kept takes, and report kind as a third number.

Then, in order of what each should give for its cost:

1. **Press to mark.** Holding the mouse button while moving makes the stroke's start and end certain, and cutting the path into strokes is where most misreadings begin (a hand on its way read as a loop, D190). Hover stays read as now, for people who never press. The legend teaches it in one line. Largest gain, smallest change.
2. **Show what was read, as it is read.** D184 draws a ring where the take sees the pointer. Draw the box or line Pinch made of the stroke, within half a second, so a person sees a miss and makes the gesture again. The reading then corrects itself at the source.
3. **Snap to what is there.** The text boxes are already read (D166). Add the edges of pictures, buttons and cards from the image, and move a rough circle to the nearest thing it mostly covers. A loose circle then gives a tight mark.
4. **Let the words vote.** "These" means more than one spot; "margin", "gap" and "between" mean a strip; "this colour" means a point; "this slide" already means the screen (D188). Decide the gesture and the words together instead of the pointer first (D185).
5. **Rests as spots.** "Remove the red dots", resting on each of five, kept only the circled one (D190, not done). A rest of 450 ms or more inside a pointing sentence, on a thing found in step 3, is a spot.
6. **Finer samples.** Read `getCoalescedEvents()` on `pointermove` so a quick circle on a trackpad has enough points to close.
7. **A recogniser in place of thresholds, once there is data.** The fifteen constants (`CIRCLE_TURN`, `CIRCLE_CLOSES`, `SCRUB_TURNS` and the rest) were set on one hand. With a few hundred labelled strokes from section 3, a template matcher of the $1 family or a small model on stroke features should replace them. Not before the data: with 38 strokes it would only learn the author.

**The bar:** nine marks in ten on the thing meant, on takes from people who are not the author. Until then the pointer is in the product and not in the headline.

## 5. A licence (point 3)

Not legal advice; `/terms` and any contract want a lawyer's read.

- **The repository stays closed and says so.** Add a `LICENSE` that reserves all rights to the author or their company, and name the owner in `package.json`. With the reading on a server, closed is the simplest and strongest position.
- **If the code is ever shown,** for trust or for a client: the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1) lets people read and run it but not compete with it, and turns into Apache 2.0 after two years. The Business Source License does much the same with more to fill in. Decide when there is a reason; nothing has to be published now.
- **What the person gets in a package.** Their words and pictures are theirs. `pinch-check.md` and the intro of `notes.md` carry one line: for use in the person's own projects, not for resale or for another product. `/terms` says the same.
- **Other people's work inside Pinch.** Whisper, Silero and the library that runs them are permissively licensed (to confirm for each, with every other dependency, in one pass with a licence checker) and need their notices kept.
- **Client work.** A contract for a harness project should say who owns what: the client owns what is theirs (their brand rules, layouts, content); the author keeps the engine and method and licenses them to the client. Check what the Guesty agreement says before the deck harness is shown or reused.
- **The name.** Consider registering "Pinch" as a mark for this class before spending on the brand.

## 6. Privacy, after Wispr Flow (point 9)

Their page works because it gives controls, not assurances: "You choose what Wispr Flow keeps, what it learns from, and when it listens." Three things to take, one to avoid.

**Take.**
1. **Separate switches for separate questions,** each with what on and off mean:
   - *Where a take is read*: on this computer, or on Pinch's server (stage 3; stage 1 has no switch and says so plainly).
   - *Understand my notes*: close-ups go to Anthropic to say what each mark is on; off, they never leave.
   - *Keep my takes*: in the account, for every project and device; off, only in this browser.
   - *Help improve Pinch*: share takes (words and pointer, never audio or pictures) for the kept set. Off unless switched on.
2. **One take, followed step by step:** you talk, the words are read here, the words and the pointer go to Pinch, notes come back, nothing is kept. Four steps with what moves at each.
3. **Plain promises that are true:** nothing is recorded until you press Space; no one is paid with your data; the model provider does not train on it and does not keep it (to be true, this needs the provider's zero-retention terms in place first).

**Avoid.** Their pages disagree with each other: one says dictation "stays on your device" where transcription always runs in the cloud, and one says zero retention where the policy says "generally deleted within 30 days". Pinch's page says where each thing is *processed* and where it is *kept*, as two columns, and no claim goes up that the code does not hold. No badges: Pinch has no audit to show.

The page lives at `/privacy` (new), and the dialog in the tool links to it.

**Pillar one: it works.** What can be said today, and what must be said with it:

| --- | --- | --- |
| Corrections that come back | 4% with Pinch, 74% without | the author's own take |
| A review that picks what you would | 70% against 32% | 28 blind picks by the author |

A new `/evidence` page holds each test as it was run: the question, the rule for the verdict set before the run (`hand-vs-pinch.md` already works this way, and that is rare enough to show), the count, what was not measured. Then run it on five to ten outside designers; one outside result is worth more on the page than all three rows above.

**Pillar two: it is fast.** The author's point is right and unmeasured: even with dictation, marking a picture by hand and then talking about it, again and again, is the slow part.

- **The unit.** Not "npm": every developer reads that as the package manager. Say it whole: **pointed notes a minute**, a note counting only when it carries a mark. On the 220-slide review that is 6.5 notes and 7.8 marks a minute.
- **The test.** The same twenty screenshots, three ways, in turned order, by at least three people, timed from the first screenshot to a package an agent can read: (a) Pinch; (b) dictation plus marking in a drawing tool; (c) typing plus marking. Count notes with a mark, and count clicks.
- **The line on the page** comes from the result: "N times faster than marking by hand", with the three bars under it. The one measured ratio so far is corrections in 4.9 minutes against 13.9 written, 2.8 times, and it compared against typing without marks.

### For companies: the harness as a service (point 4)

**What is offered,** in the deck harness's own terms: an agent that builds on-brand work from approved parts, checks it against the brand's rules, looks at what it made and fixes what fails, up to three rounds. The Guesty harness is the proof: layouts whose slots the agent fills so that sizes, colours and grids come from the system and not the model; checks on the spec and on the rendered page; an audit for decks made by hand; export to editable slides.

**Where Pinch fits in it:** the company's design lead talks through real work once, and that take becomes the rules and the check. The Guesty rules were read this way from 271 notes on 134 slides.

**On the site.** One section on the landing, "For companies", and a page at `/harness`:
- what a project is, in three steps: your lead reviews real work with Pinch; we build the harness on your design system; your team asks an agent for work that passes;
- the Guesty case with before and after, and two or three numbers from its checks;
- what it takes: a design system, a lead for two hours, three to six weeks;
- one button that opens an email or a booking page. No form that posts to Pinch's server: the privacy rule keeps emails out of the logs.

**Before any of it goes up:** Guesty's written yes to being named and shown, and the ownership point in section 5.

**Price,** as a place to start and the author's to set: a fixed pilot on one kind of work (decks, landing pages, emails), then a monthly fee to keep and extend it. The pilot should be priced as a project for a company, not as a tool: the Guesty work is months of engineering, and a five-figure pilot is the right order.

## 8. The market (point 11)

| | What it is | Captures taste by | Agent gets it by | Price | What it lacks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Taste** (buildwithtaste.com) | A library of 2,000+ real designs, a profile, a marketplace | Saving, swiping, a short note on a reference | MCP, free | Free; Pro $12.50 a month | No check of what the agent made; notes sit on a whole reference, not a spot; no voice; no evidence on the site |
| **taste-skill** (senlindesign, MIT, 468 stars) | A Claude Code skill | Reading one public website: measures, patterns, four principles | Rules files for nine tools | Free | A brand's site, not a person; no corrections kept; checks its own text, not the output |
| **"Teaching AI to Taste"** (Briscione) | An essay on food, not design | n/a | n/a | n/a | Its point carries over: tools generate and cannot select; the value is in the layer that judges |
| **A video on a paper about scientific taste** (the author's transcript) | A small judge trained on about 700,000 pairs of similar papers, one cited far more than the other | Community feedback (citations), not criteria written by hand | n/a | n/a | It models a community's taste, not one person's. Figures below are from a spoken transcript and are not checked against the paper |
| Pointing tools (Agentation, React Grab, Cursor's Design Mode, Vercel's toolbar; from [taste-harness.md](taste-harness.md)) | A selector or screenshot for one fix | A click | Context for one task | Mostly free | Nothing kept; nothing checked |

**What this says.**
- **Taste is the nearest, and it works the other way.** It collects what you love from other people's work, slowly. Pinch records what is wrong with your own work, fast, on the spot. They sell a library; Pinch sells a correction and a check. Say this difference in one line on the site.
- **Their free MCP sets the price of "your agent reads your taste" at nothing,** and of the profile at $12.50. So Pinch's paid line cannot be keeping and serving notes alone. It is the reading that understands and the check that runs, which neither product has.
- **Nobody shows evidence.** Taste's pages have no numbers on outcomes; the skill has none. A page of tests set in advance is open ground.
- **The essay's word for the gap is "evaluation layer",** and it is the same gap: everyone generates, nobody checks against a person.

**What the paper adds** (as the video tells it: the trained judge beat two large general models at picking the more-cited paper of 728 pairs, held up on fields it was not trained on, and its ideas were preferred 81% of the time over an untrained model's).
- **Pairs are an accepted measure of taste.** Pinch's own test is the same shape: a judge picks between two screens and is scored against the person's blind picks (70% against 32%). `/evidence` can say so and point to this line of work.
- **A judge with the right examples beats a much larger model without them.** That is Pinch's claim in small: the notes, not the model, carry the taste. It is also the case for the kept takes of section 3 as the thing worth owning.
- **Judging improves making.** The 81% is the argument for a check before the person sees a screen (D174), from another field.
- **It does not show one person's taste can be learned from picks.** For single designers, judges scored 54% to 60% (DesignPref, in [taste-harness.md](taste-harness.md)), which is why picks stayed out of v1. Pinch gets to one person through their words on a spot, not through their picks.
- **A company's taste is a community's.** Many people, many decks, some approved and some sent back: that is the paper's setting, and it is the harness service's, not the free tool's. Approved against rejected work is a set of pairs a client already has. Worth one line on `/harness`, and worth trying on Guesty's decks before it is promised.
- **A line for the site:** "Taste can be taught." It answers the common claim that models have none, and Pinch has its own numbers to put under it.

**Not read.** Taste's `/privacy`, `/docs/annotations` and `/docs/review-ai-generated-ui` were not fetched; the last may bear on "no check" and is worth a look before the comparison goes public. The paper itself was not read, only the transcript, which is garbled in places (the model's size, the names of the models it beat): find the paper before quoting any figure from it.

## Not known

- Whether a served check holds up in Cursor and Codex (D174 lists it as not known).
- What stage 1 adds in seconds on a long take, and what the pass costs a note.
- Whether the numbers hold for anyone but the author. This is the largest unknown in the plan, and steps 1, 4 and 8 exist to answer it.
- How much of the speed gain is pointing and how much is talking; the three-way test separates them.
