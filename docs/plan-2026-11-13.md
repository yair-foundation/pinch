# Plan: the harness as protected IP, a paid service, and a provable claim

2026-11-13. A plan: nothing here is built. The choices it turned on were made the same day and are recorded as D197 and D198 in [decisions.md](decisions.md); each stage still gets its own entry when it is built. It answers the eleven points the author asked for, in the order that builds: first what the one big improvement is, then the server, the licence, the pointer, the website and the services offer.

## The short version

1. **The best single improvement is a meaning pass.** Every note leaves Pinch able to stand alone: what was pointed at, what kind of thing it is, whether the note objects, approves or asks, and how sure Pinch is. Today half the notes are five words or fewer and mean nothing without the picture (137 of 271, counted by the agent that read the 220-slide review).
2. **All of the reading runs on a server, and that is the IP.** The browser records; the server reads the voice, the pointer and the notes. Code that never ships cannot be copied, and nobody downloads 570 MB.
3. **Every change is scored on a kept set of real takes.** The set, with its labels, is the part a competitor cannot rebuild from reading the product.
4. **Money comes from two places:** harness projects for companies (now, on the deck harness built for a client) and one paid plan for Pinch itself: the whole product free for the first 50 notes, then paid to go on (D198).
5. **The website claims two things, each with a number and its method:** fewer corrections with Pinch than without, and more pointed notes a minute than marking by hand.

## 1. The one improvement that raises the bar (points 6 and 8)

**"Pinch understands what you mean, not just what you say"** is not true yet, and the feedback of the agent that read the author's 220-slide review (`docs/reviews/pinch-package-feedback-2026-11-13.md` in the deck harness) says exactly where:

- 137 of 271 notes are five words or fewer: "Avoid this", "Off-brand", "Same here".
- 149 of 328 spots have no text under them, so the picture is the only source.
- The "Not sure" list flagged nine fragments and missed the six notes that were really misheard ("Too much more, Gene" for too much margin).
- Approval and objection looked the same. Word lists took 150 of 289 notes as saying what works; 4 of the 21 it was sure of were right (D195).
- One note carried five claims and two spots.

Word lists have reached their ceiling: `voice-notes.ts` already holds sets for praise, fault, wishes, negation, fillers and danglers, and each real take finds new exceptions. The step up is to read each note **with its picture in view**.

**The meaning pass.** For each note, the pass has the words as said, the note before and after, the gesture, the text and colour under the spot and, where there is no text, the close-up. It gives:

| Field | Example | Fixes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | "the filled coral button, top right, 'Book a demo'" | spots with no text; "this", "here", "same here" |
| Kind | button, title, picture, icon, logo, margin, empty area | "say what kind of thing is there" |
| Stance | objection, approval, question, correction of note 3 | approval lost among objections |
| The claim, whole | "This button's colour is off-brand" from "off-brand" | notes that mean nothing alone |
| One claim to one spot | five claims split across their two spots | claims that lost their spot |
| Sure or not, and why | "margin, Gene" is not a phrase; likely "margin" | the wrong "Not sure" list |

The words as said always stay beside it (D157, D168): the pass adds, it never replaces.

**Ask, don't guess.** Where the pass is unsure, the end card asks the author before Export: the close-up, what Pinch heard, what it thinks was meant, one key to accept and one to say it again. A person confirming takes a second and is right; a model guessing is neither. This is the cheapest part and may be worth building first, on the fragments already flagged.

**How we know it worked.** Three numbers on the kept takes (section 3): notes a fresh agent can act on without opening a picture (today about half); stance right (today near chance); misheard notes caught (today 0 of 6 on that take). Then the taste harness again: corrections back, and changes asked for a screen.

**What it costs.** About a twentieth of a cent a note (section 9). The judgements on the words go to Jev, and a close-up goes to a small vision model only where there is no text under the spot. Which model that is gets chosen on the kept takes, not here (D200). Since D198 there is one Pinch and the pass is part of it for everyone, so the page says what leaves before the first recording.

## 2. The harness as deep IP, and the reading on the server (points 1 and 2)

**What can and cannot be protected.**

| Thing | Protectable? | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reading a take (pieces, strokes, gesture to sentence, notes) | Yes | Runs on the server; never shipped |
| The meaning pass and its prompts | Yes | Server only |
| Which notes apply to a screen (retrieval) | Yes | Server only, behind the agent key |
| The kept takes and their labels | Yes | Never published; the strongest of the four |
| The check's instructions | Partly | Served per request with a key, versioned, metered. A person can still log what their agent receives |
| The package's format | No | It lands on the person's disk. Not worth defending |
| The capture UI | No | It is in the browser. Not worth defending |

Obfuscating browser code is not on the list: it slows a copy by a day and costs upkeep for good.

**One Pinch, read in the cloud (D198).** The browser records the voice, the pointer's path and the screens shown, and draws the marked copies from the spots that come back. Everything else is the server's. There is no on-device reading and no second tier. Four steps, in the order they are built:

**Step A: the notes on the server.** The take as Export already writes it (`pinch-take-*.json`: pointer path, screens shown, timed words) goes to a new `POST /api/pin/read`, and notes with spots come back.
- Moves: the joining of speech pieces, `claimsOf`, `pointerOn` and `classify`, the tying of "Actually", the "Not sure" list, the layout of `notes.md`. About 2,500 lines.
- For the text under a spot and the colour at a point, the browser sends what it read (words with their boxes, a few sampled colours), not the picture.
- For this step only the browser still reads the speech, so the move can be scored on the kept takes with the same words in.
- To check first: the size of a 42-minute take's pointer path against Vercel's request limit; it may need packing.

**Step B: the voice on the server.** The recording goes up in short pieces while the person talks, is read by a hosted speech model and dropped once read. The cut at pauses (D187) moves to the server with it.
- Removes: the browser's Whisper and Silero, the 570 MB download, the need for WebGPU, and the reader on the machine running `next dev` (D136). One reader, the same words for everyone.
- Reading starts while the person is still talking, so the wait after Finish (137 seconds for 11 minutes in the browser, D187) should fall to a few seconds.
- Needs: a speech provider whose terms say it keeps nothing and trains on nothing, and a cost per minute measured on the kept takes. Hosted Whisper runs from a few cents to about 40 cents an hour of audio; to confirm when it is built.
- The kept takes are the test: the hosted reader has to match or beat the 40 whole "Delete this slide" of D187 before the browser's reader is removed.

**Step C: the meaning pass and the agent's connection.**
- The pass of section 1 runs after the reading, on close-ups the browser sends for it.
- Two new MCP tools behind the agent key: `get_notes_for_screen` (say what kind of screen and what is on it; get back only the notes that apply, which is what the deck reader asked for when 40,000 tokens of notes met a small model) and the check's brief, served with them. The person's own agent does the looking, on its own tokens: if Pinch judged every screen itself, its cost would grow with how busy the agent is, not with what the person does.
- Kept in the account: the notes as text. Because the pass makes each note stand alone, they can be served without the pictures, and screenshots stay with the person.
- The exported folder stays: it is the person's copy of their own words.

**Step D: the meter and the plan** (section 9).

**What changes in the promise.** Today: nothing of a take leaves the browser (D157, D163). After step B: your voice is sent, read and dropped; your words and the pointer's path are read on Pinch's server and kept; the close-up of each spot you point at is read and not kept; your screenshots stay in the browser. The landing, the privacy dialog, `/terms`, the landing's questions and the rule in `AGENTS.md` change in the commit that ships each step, not before.

**Reusing what exists.** The gate, agent keys, the MCP route, sign-in, Lemon Squeezy and the daily caps are built. `READING_SHOWN` hides the old reading; this plan does not bring rules back (they lost 5 of 6, D155 and D156).

## 3. The kept takes: how every change is scored

Built (D201): `scripts/kept-takes/`. A take goes out for labelling with `prepare.ts`, the author labels every remark on a local page, `label.html`, and `score.ts` scores the reading as the code stands on every labelled take, one command. Its README says how. The takes stay outside the repository, in the folder `$PINCH_KEPT_TAKES` names, since they hold the author's words.

- **What a label says,** for each remark: where it was meant (boxes drawn round each thing, the whole screen, no place, or not a note), what it does (objection, approval, mixed, question, other), whether it takes back the remark before, whether it was misheard or cut off, and, where the words don't say it, what was meant.
- **What is scored:** placed as meant; spots meant that were marked and marks on a spot meant; non-notes kept out; stance, approvals and questions found; take-backs found; misheard or cut remarks the "Not sure" list names, and how many of its names are right. What was meant is kept for the meaning pass and not scored yet.
- **A label belongs to what was said,** and finds its remark again by its words, so a later change to how a take is cut is still scored on the same labels.
- **The rule.** `score.ts --save` keeps a run; `--against` sets the next beside it and exits 1 when any line got worse. A change to the reading ships only if it doesn't.
- **What to label first,** in this order: the 220-slide review (335 remarks, about two hours; its reader's findings say what to look for), the 251-slide review (105 notes, about an hour), the 336-slide review (mostly "Delete this slide", well under an hour). The two gesture takes are already labelled by what was said over them and stay with `scripts/take-strokes.ts`.
- **A bias to know about.** Every answer starts as Pinch's reading, to save time, so a label confirmed without looking agrees with the code. The page shows Pinch's spots dashed beside the author's boxes, and the score is only as honest as the looking.
- **Picks, kept too.** The author's 28 blind picks, and every later one, go in the set with the two screens each was between. Scoring a judge against a person's picks is how this field measures taste (section 8), and the pairs cost nothing more to keep.
- **Other people.** Every number today is one hand on one Mac. Three more people, one on a mouse and two on trackpads, each recording a gesture take and a ten-minute real review, with their agreement in writing.

## 4. The pointer: from 17 of 25 to something to defend (point 5)

Scoring first, since the score in use measures the wrong thing. `take-strokes.ts` asks "was a circle called a circle?". What matters to the reader is **"is the mark on the thing that was meant, and tied to the right sentence?"**. A scrub read as a rest may still mark the right spot. Score placement (the spot covers the element meant) and tying (the right sentence) on the kept takes, and report kind as a third number.

Then, in order of what each should give for its cost:

1. **Press to mark.** Holding the mouse button while moving makes the stroke's start and end certain, and cutting the path into strokes is where most misreadings begin (a hand on its way read as a loop, D190). Hover stays read as now, for people who never press. The legend teaches it in one line. Largest gain, smallest change.
2. **Show what was read, as it is read.** D184 draws a ring where the take sees the pointer. Draw the box or line Pinch made of the stroke, within half a second, so a person sees a miss and makes the gesture again. The reading then corrects itself at the source.
3. **Snap to what is there.** The text boxes are already read (D166). Add the edges of pictures, buttons and cards from the image, and move a rough circle to the nearest thing it mostly covers. A loose circle then gives a tight mark.
4. **Let the words vote.** "These" means more than one spot; "margin", "gap" and "between" mean a strip; "this colour" means a point; "this slide" already means the screen (D188). Decide the gesture and the words together instead of the pointer first (D185).
5. **Rests as spots.** "Remove the red dots", resting on each of five, kept only the circled one (D190, not done). A rest of 450 ms or more inside a pointing sentence, on a thing found in step 3, is a spot.
6. **Finer samples.** Read `getCoalescedEvents()` on `pointermove` so a quick circle on a trackpad has enough points to close.
7. **A recogniser in place of thresholds, once there is data.** The fifteen constants (`CIRCLE_TURN`, `CIRCLE_CLOSES`, `SCRUB_TURNS` and the rest) were set on one hand. With a few hundred labelled strokes from section 3, a template matcher of the $1 family or a small model on stroke features should replace them. Not before the data: with 38 strokes it would only learn the author.

**The bar:** nine marks in ten on the thing meant, on takes from people who are not the author. Until then the pointer is in the product and not in the headline.

## 5. A licence (point 3)

Not legal advice; `/terms` and any contract want a lawyer's read.

- **The repository stays closed and says so.** Done (D199): `LICENSE` reserves all rights to Yair Cohen, and `package.json` names the owner and is `UNLICENSED`. With the reading on a server, closed is the simplest and strongest position.
- **If the code is ever shown,** for trust or for a client: the Functional Source License (FSL-1.1) lets people read and run it but not compete with it, and turns into Apache 2.0 after two years. The Business Source License does much the same with more to fill in. Decide when there is a reason; nothing has to be published now.
- **What the person gets in a package.** Their words and pictures are theirs. `pinch-check.md` and the intro of `notes.md` carry one line: for use in the person's own projects, not for resale or for another product. `/terms` says the same.
- **Other people's work inside Pinch.** Checked with `pnpm licenses list` on 2026-11-13: 151 packages, all permissive (MIT, Apache 2.0, BSD, ISC) but two families. `lightningcss` is MPL 2.0, a build tool whose own files stay open and which asks nothing of Pinch's. `sharp`'s libvips is LGPL 3.0, a library the server loads and Pinch does not hand out. Neither limits a closed, hosted product. Their notices are kept by the packages themselves.
- **Client work.** A contract for a harness project should say who owns what: the client owns what is theirs (their brand rules, layouts, content); the author keeps the engine and method and licenses them to the client. Check what the existing client agreement says about confidentiality and ownership before the deck harness is described or reused, even with no name on it.
- **The name.** Consider registering "Pinch" as a mark for this class before spending on the brand.

## 6. Privacy, after Wispr Flow (point 9)

Their page works because it follows one dictation step by step and says plain things. Pinch takes that and leaves the rest: no switches (D198: one product, no data controls), no badges, no enterprise terms.

**One table, three questions for each thing.**

| | Leaves the browser | Kept | Used to make Pinch read better |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Your voice | Yes, to be read | No: dropped once read | No |
| Your words | Yes, to Pinch and to Jev for the judgements | Yes, as your notes | Yes |
| The pointer's path | Yes | Yes, with the take | Yes |
| The close-up of a spot you point at | Yes, where there is no text under it, to a model that says what it is | By Pinch, no. By the model's host: nothing, or up to 30 days for abuse checks, depending on the host chosen (section 9) | No |
| Your screenshots | No | No | No |

**One take, followed step by step:** you press Space and talk; the voice goes up, is read and dropped; the words and the pointer become notes; each close-up is read to say what the mark is on; the notes are kept and the folder is yours.

**Plain lines that the code has to hold:** nothing is recorded until you press Space; screenshots never leave; nobody is paid with your data; the speech and model providers train on nothing, and keep nothing where their terms say so (the page says what each keeps, not more); deleting the account deletes the notes and takes them out of what Pinch learns from.

**What "used to make Pinch read better" means.** Pinch has no model of its own to train. Words and pointer paths go into the kept set of section 3, which is how the reading is scored and its thresholds set. There is no switch for it, so the page says it before the first recording, in one line, not in the terms.

**Two cautions.** Designers' screenshots are often client work under NDA, which is why they never leave and why the line about them goes first. And keeping people's words for this purpose without a switch needs a lawyer's read for visitors in the EU before it ships.

**Avoid** what their pages get wrong: one says dictation "stays on your device" where transcription always runs in the cloud, and one says zero retention where the policy says "generally deleted within 30 days". No claim goes up that the code does not hold.

The page lives at `/privacy` (new), and the dialog in the tool links to it.

**Pillar two: it is fast.** The author's point is right and unmeasured: even with dictation, marking a picture by hand and then talking about it, again and again, is the slow part.

- **The unit.** Not "npm": every developer reads that as the package manager. Say it whole: **pointed notes a minute**, a note counting only when it carries a mark. On the 220-slide review that is 6.5 notes and 7.8 marks a minute.

- **The line on the page** comes from the result: "N times faster than marking by hand", with the three bars under it. The one measured ratio so far is corrections in 4.9 minutes against 13.9 written, 2.8 times, and it compared against typing without marks.

### For companies: the harness as a service (point 4)

**What is offered,** in the deck harness's own terms: an agent that builds on-brand work from approved parts, checks it against the brand's rules, looks at what it made and fixes what fails, up to three rounds. The deck harness built for one client is the proof, shown with no client named: layouts whose slots the agent fills so that sizes, colours and grids come from the system and not the model; checks on the spec and on the rendered page; an audit for decks made by hand; export to editable slides.

**Where Pinch fits in it:** the company's design lead talks through real work once, and that take becomes the rules and the check. That client's rules were read this way from 271 notes on 134 slides.

**On the site.** One section on the landing, "For companies", and a page at `/harness`:
- what a project is, in three steps: your lead reviews real work with Pinch; we build the harness on your design system; your team asks an agent for work that passes;
- the case with before and after, and two or three numbers from its checks. The client is not named and does not matter; the work does. So nothing of theirs is on the page: no logo, name, brand colour, typeface, photo or slide. The examples are drawn again on a made-up brand, built by the same harness;
- what it takes: a design system, a lead for two hours, three to six weeks;
- one button that opens an email or a booking page. No form that posts to Pinch's server: the privacy rule keeps emails out of the logs.

**Before any of it goes up:** a read of the client agreement (section 5). Leaving the name off does not by itself settle what may be said about the work.

**Price,** as a place to start and the author's to set: a fixed pilot on one kind of work (decks, landing pages, emails), then a monthly fee to keep and extend it. The pilot should be priced as a project for a company, not as a tool: the deck harness is months of engineering, and a five-figure pilot is the right order.

**What this says.**
- **Taste is the nearest, and it works the other way.** It collects what you love from other people's work, slowly. Pinch records what is wrong with your own work, fast, on the spot. They sell a library; Pinch sells a correction and a check. Say this difference in one line on the site.
- **Their free MCP sets the price of "your agent reads your taste" at nothing,** and of the profile at $12.50. Pinch does not answer with a thinner free tier: it is one product, whole, free for the first 50 notes and paid after (D198). What it has that neither has is the reading that understands and the check.
- **Nobody shows evidence.** Taste's pages have no numbers on outcomes; the skill has none. A page of tests set in advance is open ground.
- **The essay's word for the gap is "evaluation layer",** and it is the same gap: everyone generates, nobody checks against a person.

**What the paper adds** (as the video tells it: the trained judge beat two large general models at picking the more-cited paper of 728 pairs, held up on fields it was not trained on, and its ideas were preferred 81% of the time over an untrained model's).
- **Pairs are an accepted measure of taste.** Pinch's own test is the same shape: a judge picks between two screens and is scored against the person's blind picks (70% against 32%). `/evidence` can say so and point to this line of work.
- **A judge with the right examples beats a much larger model without them.** That is Pinch's claim in small: the notes, not the model, carry the taste. It is also the case for the kept takes of section 3 as the thing worth owning.
- **Judging improves making.** The 81% is the argument for a check before the person sees a screen (D174), from another field.
- **It does not show one person's taste can be learned from picks.** For single designers, judges scored 54% to 60% (DesignPref, in [taste-harness.md](taste-harness.md)), which is why picks stayed out of v1. Pinch gets to one person through their words on a spot, not through their picks.
- **A company's taste is a community's.** Many people, many decks, some approved and some sent back: that is the paper's setting, and it is the harness service's, not the free tool's. Approved against rejected work is a set of pairs a client already has. Worth one line on `/harness`, and worth trying on the first client's decks before it is promised.
- **A line for the site:** "Taste can be taught." It answers the common claim that models have none, and Pinch has its own numbers to put under it.

**Not read.** Taste's `/privacy`, `/docs/annotations` and `/docs/review-ai-generated-ui` were not fetched; the last may bear on "no check" and is worth a look before the comparison goes public. The paper itself was not read, only the transcript, which is garbled in places (the model's size, the names of the models it beat): find the paper before quoting any figure from it.

## 9. One Pinch, metered (D198)

**The model.** No Free and Pro. Everyone gets the whole product. The first **50 notes** are free, counted over as many takes and days as they take, never reset; after that Pinch is paid, **$15 a month** (D200; it was $19 in D198), with no count. A take that starts under the limit always finishes and exports. The first take needs no account and is counted by the browser; signing in carries the count to the account. Both numbers are settings, not constants.

**Why notes.** A note is what the person makes and what the agent uses, it is the unit the site will boast of (pointed notes a minute), and Pinch's cost rises with it.

**Where 50 comes from.** The question was which number makes paying most likely. Nobody has met a paywall in Pinch, so no number here is measured on payment. What the record does show is where the value is complete, and where the one real person said they would go on.

| Evidence | Notes | What happened |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| The learning curve, three made-up designers ([taste-harness.md](taste-harness.md)) | 8 | Changes a screen fall from 11.7 to 6.7: 43% fewer |
| | 15 | 4.7: 58% fewer |
| | 22 | 4.3: 62% fewer, and flat from here to 35 |

- **The gain is nearly whole after two takes of corrections and whole after three.** In made-up remarks that is 22 notes. At the rate a real person's talk splits, about 15 a round, three rounds are 45.
- **So the floor is about 45.** Under it, some people meet the wall before they have seen a correction stay made, which is the thing they would be paying for.
- **The ceiling is about 100.** That is where the only real person was when they said they would go on, having done a first take and two rounds. Past it every note is given away after the case is made.
- **50 sits just over the floor.** Someone who starts with corrections gets three rounds and the start of a fourth. Someone who starts with a small first take (five screenshots, about 30 notes) gets it and two rounds. Both meet the wall having seen the curve, at the moment they reach for the next round.
- **Where 50 is weak.** A first take as large as the author's, 65 notes, uses the whole allowance before any correction. That person has a whole review in hand and meets the wall at their first correction. If the first accounts mostly look like that, the number goes up.

**How to find out.** Count, per account and with nothing said: notes at each take, and whether the wall was met and passed. After the first fifty accounts, move the number to where people who went on to pay were when they met it.

**Which model reads the notes: not settled, and not one model** (D200). The pass sits behind one small interface, so the model is a setting, and it is chosen by running the candidates on the kept takes.

- **The judgements on the words go to Jev** (TypeSafe), which Pinch already calls for tags (`src/lib/jev.ts`, D66). It returns typed answers with probabilities and writes no text: a choice for stance, a yes or no for "this takes back the note before" and "this was misheard", a choice among the spots for which claim goes where. The probabilities are the "sure or not" the pass needs. It is not trained on requests and runs in the United States; zero retention is for enterprise customers.
- **The description is built, not written:** kind, colour, place and the text under the spot go into a template ("the coral button, top right, 'Book a demo'"). So no model has to generate prose, and nothing can be invented.
- **The close-up needs eyes only where there is no text under the spot.** Jev takes images, but TypeSafe calls that experimental and does not vouch for it in production, so it is one candidate among the others:

- **Why not Kimi.** It is a flagship model at twenty times the price of the small ones, its maker no longer sells a cheap model that sees, and its own terms let content be used to improve the service unless agreed otherwise in writing. Hosted in the United States the terms are fine and the price is the same. Nothing found says it reads a UI crop better than the small models.
- **How it is chosen.** None of the cheap models differs from another by more than a fiftieth of a cent a note, and no independent benchmark of them on screenshots was found. So: accuracy on the kept takes first (the kind of thing at 149 spots a person has labelled), then what the provider keeps, then price.
- **What the provider keeps decides what the page may say.** "Read and not kept" is true only on a host with zero retention switched on. Anthropic and OpenAI keep requests up to 30 days for abuse checks unless their sales teams agree otherwise, so with either the page says that, in those words.

**Do large files cost more?** No. What costs is notes and minutes of talk.
- Screenshots never upload, so their size and number cost nothing: 336 slides with 43 notes cost as 43 notes.
- The model is sent a crop, made small in the browser to a fixed size before it goes, so a 20 MB screenshot costs the same tokens as a small one.
- Audio is billed by its length and dropped: an hour is four cents.
- What is kept is words and the pointer's path, a megabyte or two for the longest take before packing.

**The speech provider: Groq, whisper-large-v3-turbo.**
- It is the model the browser reads with today (D161), so the kept takes compare like with like.
- It gives a time for every word, which tying a gesture to a sentence needs.
- Its page says "By default, Groq does not retain customer data for inference requests", and "All customers may enable Zero Data Retention (ZDR) in Data Controls settings". Without that setting, logs for reliability and abuse can be kept up to 30 days. So the setting goes on before any audio does.
- **Not yet shown:** no line on the pages read says outright that it trains on nothing; its data addendum limits use to providing the service. That gets confirmed in writing before the site says "trains on nothing". Data sits in the United States, which the lawyer should know.
- OpenAI says outright that API data is not trained on and lists no retention for transcription, but its zero-retention setting needs approval, it costs nine times as much, and it is a different model. Deepgram's retention terms could not be read. If Groq's terms fall short, the fallback is the same model on a rented GPU, where nothing is kept because nothing else is there.

**People who paid $19 under D81** have Pinch with no count, for good. They are few, they paid for a product whose features are now hidden, and an end date would be one more thing to build.

**Not offered** (D198): single sign-on and SAML, SCIM, audit logs, managed deployment, HIPAA, an MSA or DPA, data controls. A company that needs those is a harness project, not a plan.
